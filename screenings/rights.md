# Pre-launch rights & compliance check list

Scope: consumer apps / SaaS / browser extensions built by a solo founder, selling or giving access to people in
Canada (federal + Quebec), the US (federal + California), the EU and the UK. Researched 2026-10-05.

> **Not legal advice.** This list helps Claude spot likely problems in a source folder before launch. It cannot
> confirm that a product is compliant. Every item marked **blocker** should be checked by a lawyer in the
> relevant jurisdiction before launch, and so should anything involving children, health/financial data,
> or paid subscriptions. Laws below change often; dates were correct as of 2026-10-05.

**Severity:** blocker = do not launch until fixed (or lawyer signs off) · high = fix before launch ·
med = fix within the first weeks · low = good practice / tidy-up.

**How Claude runs it:** walk the repo (routes, templates, `package.json`/lockfiles, `<script>` tags,
`manifest.json`, email templates, assets folders, copy files, pricing config), mark each row
Pass / Fail / N/A / Needs human, and quote the file and line for each Fail. "Needs human" is for anything that
depends on facts outside the repo (company registration, contracts with vendors, real reviews).

| 6 Apr 2025 | UK DMCC Act unfair-practices rules (fake reviews banned, drip pricing) in force |
| 28 Jun 2025 | European Accessibility Act applies (services; micro-enterprises <10 staff and ≤€2M exempt for services) |

| 19 Jun 2026 | EU "withdrawal button" (Directive 2023/2673) for online distance contracts |

## Checks

| id | category | check | law / rule (jurisdiction) | how to detect in a repo | severity | fix |

| privacy-policy-exists | Legal documents | A privacy policy exists, is reachable from every page/app screen and from the store listing, and is dated | GDPR Art. 13–14 (EU/UK); PIPEDA Principle 8 Openness (CA); Law 25 s.3.2 (QC); CCPA §1798.130 / CalOPPA (US-CA); Chrome Web Store / App Store policies | Look for `app/privacy`, `pages/privacy*`, `/privacy-policy`, `privacy.html`; grep footer/layout components for a link (`href=".*privacy`); check `manifest.json`/store listing config for a privacy URL; check for a "last updated" date | blocker | Add a policy page linked from footer, signup, settings and store listing; date it |
| privacy-policy-accurate | Legal documents | The policy matches what the code actually does: every data type collected, purpose, legal basis (EU/UK), recipients/vendors, retention, transfers, rights, contact | GDPR Art. 13; Law 25 s.8; CCPA "notice at collection"; FTC Act s.5 deception (US) | Diff the data inventory you build from the code (form fields, DB schema/`prisma/schema.prisma`, `fetch` targets, SDKs in `package.json`) against the policy text; flag any SDK or field not mentioned; flag "we never share" if any third-party call exists | blocker | Rewrite the policy from the real data inventory; list each vendor and purpose |
| terms-of-service | Legal documents | Terms exist and cover: who the contract is with, acceptable use, IP/licence to user content, paid plans, termination, liability limits, governing law, how changes are notified | Contract law; Quebec Consumer Protection Act (abusive clauses, s.11.2 change-notice rules); EU Unfair Contract Terms Directive 93/13; UK CRA 2015 Part 2 | Look for `app/terms`, `/tos`, `terms.html`; check signup flow references it; grep for "binding arbitration", "class action waiver", "we may change these terms at any time" | high | Add terms; for EU/UK/QC consumers drop one-sided change clauses, keep mandatory consumer rights, note local-law carve-outs |
| terms-acceptance-recorded | Legal documents | Users actively agree to terms (clickwrap) and the version/time is stored | Contract enforceability (US case law on browsewrap); GDPR accountability if consent is used | Check signup form for an unticked checkbox or "By clicking X you agree" next to the button; check DB for `terms_version`/`accepted_at` fields | med | Add clickwrap near the submit button, store version + timestamp |

| business-identity | Business identity | Legal name of the business (or the founder's name if a sole proprietor), geographic address, email contact, and registration/VAT number where applicable, shown on the site and in terms | EU E-Commerce Directive 2000/31 Art. 5; UK E-Commerce Regs 2002 reg. 6; EU CRD Art. 6; Quebec CPA s.54.4; CASL (in messages); German Impressum if targeting DE | grep footer/about/terms/legal pages for a postal address and legal entity name; flag placeholders like "Company Inc.", "123 Main St", "lorem" | high | Add a Legal/Imprint page with entity, address (a virtual office/registered agent is fine), email; reuse in emails and terms |
| contact-channel | Business identity | A working way to reach a human (email or form), not only a chatbot | EU E-Commerce Directive Art. 5(1)(c); DSA Art. 12 single point of contact for users (hosting/platforms) | Check `mailto:` targets and form handlers actually deliver (MX on domain, form posts to a real endpoint); flag `noreply@` as the only address | high | Publish a monitored address; verify MX records |
| consent-prior | Consent & cookies | No non-essential cookie, pixel, analytics, session replay, or fingerprinting fires before consent (EU/UK/QC) | ePrivacy Art. 5(3); PECR reg. 6 (UK, with DUAA 2026 narrow exemptions); Law 25 s.8.1 (tracking off by default); CNIL/EDPB guidance | Load the built site with no consent state and list cookies/network calls (`googletagmanager`, `gtag(`, `fbq(`, `posthog.init`, `mixpanel.init`, `hotjar`, `clarity`, `_ga`, `_fbp`); check init code is gated behind a consent check | blocker (EU/UK targeting) | Load SDKs only after an "accept" for that category; default all categories off; or switch to cookieless, first-party, aggregate analytics |
| consent-reject-parity | Consent & cookies | The banner has "Reject all" on the first layer, as visible and easy as "Accept all"; no pre-ticked categories; no cookie wall; consent can be withdrawn as easily as given (link in footer) | EDPB Cookie Banner Taskforce report (2023); GDPR Art. 7(3), Recital 32; ICO cookie guidance; CNIL | Inspect banner component (`CookieBanner`, `ConsentManager`, CMP config such as Cookiebot/OneTrust/Klaro): look for a reject button in the first view, button styles (same size/contrast), `defaultChecked`/`checked: true` on categories, a footer "Cookie settings" link | high | Equal-weight Accept/Reject buttons, categories off by default, persistent "Cookie settings" link |
| consent-records | Consent & cookies | Consent choices are logged (what, when, banner version) so you can prove consent | GDPR Art. 7(1); Law 25 accountability | Check where consent is stored (cookie only vs server log); look for a version field | med | Store choice + timestamp + banner version; server-side if you rely on consent for marketing |
| gpc-dnt | Consent & cookies | Global Privacy Control is honoured as an opt-out of sale/sharing, and the site shows that it was honoured | CCPA Regs §7025 (US-CA); Colorado/Connecticut etc. universal opt-out | grep for `navigator.globalPrivacyControl` or `Sec-GPC` header handling; check if ad pixels/retargeting still fire with GPC on | high (if any ad/retargeting SDK) | Read GPC on load, suppress sale/share SDKs, show "Opt-out request honoured" |
| do-not-sell-link | Consent & cookies | If you "sell" or "share" (cross-context behavioural ads) personal info, a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link (or "Your Privacy Choices") is present | CCPA §1798.135 (US-CA; applies above thresholds) | Detect ad/retargeting pixels (Meta, TikTok, Google Ads, LinkedIn Insight, Reddit pixel); check footer for the link | med (high if over CCPA thresholds) | Remove ad pixels or add the link + opt-out flow |
| form-consent-unticked | Consent & cookies | Every optional consent on forms (marketing, data sharing, research) is a separate, unticked box, not bundled with terms acceptance | GDPR Art. 4(11), 7, Recital 32 & 43; CASL express consent; Law 25 s.14 (consent separate for each purpose) | grep form components for `defaultChecked`, `checked={true}`, `checked` attribute on checkboxes; look for one checkbox covering "terms and marketing" | high | Split into separate unticked boxes, each with plain-language purpose |
| data-minimisation | Data minimisation & retention | Forms and events collect only what the feature needs (no DOB, phone, gender, address "just in case"); privacy-friendly defaults | GDPR Art. 5(1)(c), Art. 25 privacy by default; Law 25 s.9.1 (highest privacy settings by default); PIPEDA Principle 4 (limiting collection); Chrome Web Store Limited Use | List every input field and tracked event property (`track(`, `capture(`, `logEvent(`); flag ones not used anywhere downstream; check default settings (public profile, sharing) are off | high | Delete unused fields/events; make sharing opt-in |
| retention-schedule | Data minimisation & retention | Each data type has a retention period, and code actually deletes/anonymises (cron, TTL, purge job) | GDPR Art. 5(1)(e); Law 25 s.23 (destroy/anonymise when purpose achieved); PIPEDA Principle 5; COPPA §312.10 (written retention policy for kids' data) | Look for scheduled jobs (`cron`, `vercel.json` crons, `pg_cron`, TTL indexes, S3 lifecycle rules); check logs retention config; flag "we keep data forever" | med | Set periods per data type, implement purge jobs, document them in the policy |
| logs-no-pii | Data minimisation & retention | Logs, error trackers and analytics don't capture passwords, tokens, full emails, message content, or payment data | GDPR Art. 5, 32; PCI DSS (cards) | grep `console.log(`, `logger.` around auth/payment code; check Sentry `sendDefaultPii`, `beforeSend`, session replay `maskAllInputs` settings | high | Scrub/mask PII in logging and replay configs |
| third-party-sdk-audit | Third parties | Every third-party SDK, script, font CDN, embed and API is listed with what data it receives, and each is necessary | GDPR Art. 28, 44; Law 25 s.17–18 (transfers), s.18.3 (service contracts); PIPEDA Principle 1 (accountability for transfers); FTC (health/data-sharing cases) | Parse `package.json`/lockfile and `<script src>` / `<link href>` hosts / `fetch("https://` / `manifest.json` `host_permissions`; flag Google Fonts CDN, YouTube embeds, chat widgets, Meta/TikTok pixels | high | Remove what isn't needed; self-host fonts; list the rest as sub-processors |
| subprocessor-list | Third parties | A public list of sub-processors/vendors (hosting, email, payments, analytics, AI provider) with location | GDPR Art. 28(2)/(4), Art. 13(1)(e); Law 25 s.8 (third parties) | Compare vendor list built from code/env vars (`STRIPE_`, `RESEND_`, `OPENAI_`, `ANTHROPIC_`, `SUPABASE_`, `VERCEL`) to any `/subprocessors` page or policy section | med | Publish a list; note how changes are announced |
| dpa-signed | Third parties | A data processing agreement is in place with each processor (most vendors offer a click-through DPA) | GDPR Art. 28(3); UK GDPR; Law 25 s.18.3 (written contract with service provider) | Not visible in code → mark "Needs human"; list each vendor found in the SDK audit as a reminder | high | Accept each vendor's DPA in their dashboard; keep copies |

## Notes on things that are easy to get wrong

- **Click-to-cancel in the US is not dead.** The FTC rule was vacated in July 2025, but ROSCA, the FTC Act and
  state laws (California ARL since 1 Jul 2025, plus New York, Minnesota and others) still require easy online
  cancellation and clear disclosure. The FTC sent a new ANPRM for review in January 2026.
- **UK cookies after DUAA (Feb 2026):** first-party, aggregate analytics with a clear opt-out can skip consent,
  but GA4-style third-party analytics, ads and replay tools still need prior consent.
- **Quebec is stricter than the rest of Canada:** named person in charge on the website, privacy-by-default,
  PIA before transferring data outside Quebec, incident register, French versions.
- **EAA micro-enterprise exemption** covers services from companies with <10 staff and ≤€2M turnover; a solo
  founder is usually exempt, but WCAG 2.2 AA is still the US ADA litigation benchmark and cheap to meet at launch.
- **EU AI Act Art. 50** applies from 2 Aug 2026; the Digital Omnibus delayed high-risk rules, not transparency.
